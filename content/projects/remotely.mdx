Remotely lets you drive a Mac with your TV remote. I wanted to watch stuff on the TV from my chair (or my bed) without getting up for a mouse every time, and the remote was already sitting right there. It's my first macOS app, a small menu bar app for macOS 15 and later, and you can grab it from the GitHub releases page.

## What it does

The arrows move the pointer. Tap to nudge it a few pixels, hold to glide, and it speeds up the longer you hold. Center clicks, twice double-clicks and holding right-clicks. Back works as back, and tapping it twice flips the arrows into scrolling, with a little chip next to the pointer showing which mode you're in. You can rebind all of it, including to your own keyboard shortcuts.

The onboarding takes after [Alcove](https://tryalcove.com/). It goes one step at a time, shows where your TV brand hides its HDMI-CEC setting, gets the Accessibility permission, then has you practice each gesture with the real remote, so you know it works before you're done.

## How it works

TVs with HDMI-CEC already send the remote's button presses down the HDMI cable to whatever's plugged in. On a Mac, a system daemon (`corercd`) owns that connection and won't let other apps talk to it. It does write every press to the system log, though, so Remotely streams that log, reads the raw CEC button code from each line and turns it into pointer moves, clicks, scrolls and shortcuts (that's what the Accessibility permission is for). No account, no network. Updates come through [Sparkle](https://sparkle-project.org/).
