A bot that joins a Google Meet or Teams call and talks like a person. I built the first version over a few days in November 2024, right after [Transcript Seeker](/projects/transcript_seeker), and then the Meeting BaaS team took it over. They added the persona files, a proper server and a hosted API, so the repo today does a lot more than mine did. It's open source under MIT and lives in their org now.

## What my version did

You gave it a meeting link, and it joined the call and held a spoken conversation. It handled people cutting in, and I gave it a couple of toy tools, so you could ask it for the weather or the time.

## How I built it

[Meeting BaaS](https://www.meetingbaas.com/) joins the call and streams the meeting's audio over a WebSocket. My bot was a [Pipecat](https://www.pipecat.ai/) pipeline on the other end: [Deepgram](https://deepgram.com/) turned speech into text, an OpenAI model wrote the reply and [Cartesia](https://cartesia.ai/) spoke it back. The awkward part was the glue. Meeting BaaS sends plain audio and Pipecat wants its own protobuf frames, so I wrote a small proxy that wrapped the audio going in and unwrapped the replies coming out, and I had to pin the voice to the same 16 kHz as the call. A script started each bot, its proxy and its own [ngrok](https://ngrok.com/) tunnel so Meeting BaaS could reach it, which is how I got a few bots into a meeting at once.

## What the team did next

They swapped my scripts for a server that starts bots on request and added personas, which are Markdown files holding a character's traits, voice and first line. They also built the hosted API. These screenshots show their version, not mine.
