This research project looks at swapping faces to hide who was recorded in self-driving car footage, while keeping the footage useful for training driving models. The demo code swaps faces on a live webcam feed.

## How it works

The paper tries three approaches on recorded video: an OpenCV face swap with a preset AI-generated face, [MediaPipe](https://ai.google.dev/edge/mediapipe/solutions/guide) Holistic blurring, and a MediaPipe Face Mesh swap. The OpenCV swap did not work for people far away. Face Mesh only swaps when a face covers between 2% and 50% of the frame. Blurring doesn't swap anything, but Holistic became the segmentation step of the combined approach.

The combined approach segments each person, zooms the face to around 40% of the frame, swaps it with Face Mesh and applies the result as a mask on the segmented image. It repeats for each person and outputs an MP4 file. The paper reports it works well up to 15 feet away.

The code in the repository is the Face Mesh swap. Face Mesh finds landmarks on the original and replacement faces, and the code splits them into triangles. OpenCV warps each replacement triangle onto the original face, then seamless cloning blends it into the frame. The replacement face comes from [This Person Does Not Exist](https://thispersondoesnotexist.com/), so it belongs to no real person.

[AutonomousDrivingPaperCode](https://github.com/techwithanirudh/AutonomousDrivingPaperCode/) runs it on a webcam and [AutonomousDrivingPaperCodeCombined](https://github.com/techwithanirudh/AutonomousDrivingPaperCodeCombined) on a video file. The segmentation and zoom steps are in the paper but not in either repository.
