My research paper on swapping faces in self-driving car footage, so you can't tell who was recorded but the footage is still useful for training driving models. The paper is up on OSF, and the demo code (which swaps faces on a live webcam feed) is on GitHub.

## What it found

I tried three approaches on recorded video: a face swap with OpenCV, blurring with [MediaPipe](https://ai.google.dev/edge/mediapipe/solutions/guide), and a MediaPipe Face Mesh swap. The OpenCV swap didn't work for people far from the camera, and blurring hides faces but doesn't swap anything. The combined approach from the paper works well up to 15 feet away.

## How it works

The combined approach finds each person, zooms in on their face, swaps it and pastes the result back, then moves on to the next person.

<Screens>
  <Screen frame='wide' height={455} src='/images/projects/autonomous_driving/combined-pipeline.webp' width={1600} alt='Hand-drawn flow where a video goes through MediaPipe Holistic segmentation, a face zoom to about 40 percent, a Face Mesh swap and a mask step, and comes out as an MP4 file, repeated for each person' caption='The combined approach, from the paper.' />
</Screens>

The new faces come from [This Person Does Not Exist](https://thispersondoesnotexist.com/), so they don't belong to anyone real. [AutonomousDrivingPaperCode](https://github.com/techwithanirudh/AutonomousDrivingPaperCode/) runs the swap on a webcam and [AutonomousDrivingPaperCodeCombined](https://github.com/techwithanirudh/AutonomousDrivingPaperCodeCombined) runs it on a video file. Neither one has the full combined pipeline, though.
