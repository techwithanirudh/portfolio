This is a research paper on swapping faces to hide who was recorded in self-driving car footage, while keeping the footage useful for training driving models. The paper is posted on OSF, and the demo code is public on GitHub. The code swaps faces on a live webcam feed.

## What it found

The paper tries three approaches on recorded video: a face swap with OpenCV, blurring with [MediaPipe](https://ai.google.dev/edge/mediapipe/solutions/guide), and a MediaPipe Face Mesh swap. The OpenCV swap did not work for people far away. Blurring hides faces but swaps nothing. The paper's combined approach works well up to 15 feet away.

## How it works

The combined approach finds each person, zooms in on the face, swaps it and pastes the result back, then repeats for the next person.

<Screens>
  <Screen frame='wide' height={455} src='/images/projects/autonomous_driving/combined-pipeline.webp' width={1600} alt='Hand-drawn flow where a video goes through MediaPipe Holistic segmentation, a face zoom to about 40 percent, a Face Mesh swap and a mask step, and comes out as an MP4 file, repeated for each person' caption='The combined approach, from the paper.' />
</Screens>

The replacement faces come from [This Person Does Not Exist](https://thispersondoesnotexist.com/), so they belong to no real person. [AutonomousDrivingPaperCode](https://github.com/techwithanirudh/AutonomousDrivingPaperCode/) runs the swap on a webcam and [AutonomousDrivingPaperCodeCombined](https://github.com/techwithanirudh/AutonomousDrivingPaperCodeCombined) on a video file. Neither has the full combined pipeline.
